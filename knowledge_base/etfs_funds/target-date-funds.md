A target-date fund (TDF) is an all-in-one portfolio that automatically adjusts its risk as you approach a chosen retirement year. A "Target 2060 Fund" is built for someone retiring around 2060.

**How it works — the glide path:** When the target date is decades away, the fund holds ~90% stocks for growth. Each year it gradually shifts toward bonds, landing around 30–50% stocks at the target date and continuing to de-risk after. This automates the most commonly neglected tasks in personal investing: age-appropriate allocation and rebalancing.

**What's inside:** Typically a handful of broad index funds — total U.S. stock, international stock, and bond funds — wrapped in one ticker. Effectively a professionally managed three-fund portfolio.

**Strengths:**

- One decision, then truly hands-off for decades.
- Automatic rebalancing and de-risking, immune to panic and procrastination.
- Default option in most 401(k) plans for good reason.

**Watch-outs:**

- **Fees vary widely.** Index-based TDFs (Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab) charge 0.08–0.15%; some actively managed TDFs charge 0.5%+ for similar outcomes.
- **Use it alone.** Mixing a TDF with other stock funds defeats its calibrated allocation.
- **Date ≠ mandate.** Choose the date matching your *risk tolerance*, not just your age — picking a later date keeps you more aggressive, an earlier one more conservative.

For a beginner unsure where to start, a low-cost TDF is arguably the single best default answer.
