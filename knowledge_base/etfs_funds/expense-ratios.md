---
title: "Expense Ratios: The Silent Return Killer"
category: etfs_funds
source: Adapted from SEC investor.gov fee guidance and Morningstar research
---

The expense ratio is a fund's annual operating fee, expressed as a percentage of your investment and deducted automatically from returns. A 0.50% expense ratio costs $50 per year on a $10,000 holding — you never see a bill; performance is simply lower.

**Why small percentages matter enormously:** Fees compound against you exactly as returns compound for you. On a $100,000 portfolio growing 7% annually for 30 years:

- At a 0.05% fee → final value ≈ $750,000
- At a 1.00% fee → final value ≈ $574,000

That seemingly tiny 0.95% difference consumed about **$176,000 — nearly a quarter of the potential gain.**

**Benchmarks for judging a fund's cost:**

- Broad index ETFs/funds: 0.02–0.10% (excellent)
- Specialty/sector index funds: 0.10–0.40% (acceptable)
- Actively managed funds: 0.50–1.50% (a high hurdle for the manager to overcome)
- Anything above 1.5%: rarely justified

**Morningstar's famous finding:** the expense ratio is the single most reliable predictor of future fund performance — more predictive than past returns or star ratings. Low cost doesn't guarantee success, but high cost reliably predicts underperformance.

Also watch for fees outside the expense ratio: sales loads, account maintenance fees, and advisory "wrap" fees of ~1% that stack on top of fund costs.
