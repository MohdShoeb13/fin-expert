title: "Index Funds: The Case for Owning the Whole Market"

---

An index fund doesn't try to pick winners — it simply buys every security in a market index at market weight, aiming to match the market's return at the lowest possible cost. Pioneered by Vanguard founder John Bogle in 1976, index investing is now the most widely recommended strategy for individual investors.

**Why such a humble strategy wins:**

1. **Markets are hard to beat.** Prices already reflect the analysis of millions of professionals. After fees, 85–90% of active funds trail their index over 15 years (SPIVA data).
2. **Costs compound.** An index fund charging 0.04% versus an active fund charging 1% saves nearly 1% per year — roughly 25% more total wealth over a 40-year horizon.
3. **No manager risk.** You'll never suffer a star manager's cold streak or departure.
4. **Simplicity.** A three-fund portfolio (total U.S. stock + total international stock + total bond) is a complete, professional-grade allocation.

**The guaranteed bargain:** An index fund guarantees you the market's return minus ~0.05%. Active funds offer a small chance of beating the market and a large chance of trailing it by 1%+ annually.

**What index funds don't do:** protect you in crashes (you own the market, you ride its falls), or beat the market (matching it *is* the goal — which, paradoxically, places you ahead of most professionals over time).
