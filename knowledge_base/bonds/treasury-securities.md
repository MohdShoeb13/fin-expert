U.S. Treasury securities are debt issued by the federal government — considered the lowest-credit-risk investments in the world because they are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States.

**The lineup by maturity:**

- **Treasury bills (T-bills):** 4 weeks to 1 year. Sold at a discount; no coupon — your return is the difference between purchase price and face value. A common cash-parking vehicle.
- **Treasury notes:** 2 to 10 years, paying interest every six months. The 10-year note's yield is the benchmark that influences mortgage and loan rates economy-wide.
- **Treasury bonds:** 20 to 30 years, semiannual interest. Highest interest-rate sensitivity.
- **TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities):** Principal adjusts with CPI inflation, protecting real purchasing power.
- **I Savings Bonds:** Inflation-linked savings bonds bought directly (limit $10,000/person/year); rate combines a fixed and an inflation component.

**Tax note:** Treasury interest is subject to federal income tax but **exempt from state and local taxes** — a meaningful advantage in high-tax states.

**Role in a portfolio:** Treasuries are the classic "flight to safety" asset, often rising when stocks crash. Short-term Treasuries behave almost like cash; long-term Treasuries are volatile but provide the strongest diversification against equity bear markets driven by recession.
