---
title: What Is a Bond?
category: bonds
source: Adapted from SEC investor.gov and FINRA bond basics
---

A bond is a loan from you to a government, municipality, or corporation. The issuer promises to pay periodic interest (the **coupon**) and to repay the face value (**principal**, typically $1,000 per bond) on a set date (**maturity**).

**Anatomy of a bond:** A 10-year Treasury with a 4% coupon pays $40 per year per $1,000 bond, then returns the $1,000 at maturity.

**Key concepts:**

- **Coupon rate:** The fixed annual interest as a percentage of face value.
- **Yield:** Your actual return, which depends on the price you paid. Buying below face value ("at a discount") raises your yield above the coupon; buying above lowers it.
- **Maturity:** Short-term (under 3 years), intermediate (3–10), long-term (10–30). Longer maturities mean more sensitivity to interest rates.
- **Credit quality:** Rating agencies (Moody's, S&P) grade issuers from AAA (safest) down through junk/"high yield." Lower quality must pay higher yields to attract buyers.

**Why investors hold bonds:** predictable income, much lower volatility than stocks, and diversification — high-quality bonds often hold or gain value when stocks fall, cushioning the portfolio.

**Practical access:** Most individuals own bonds through bond funds and ETFs rather than individual bonds, gaining instant diversification across hundreds of issuers. Treasuries can also be bought directly via TreasuryDirect.gov.
