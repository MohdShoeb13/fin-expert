---
title: Bond Funds vs. Individual Bonds
category: bonds
source: Adapted from Bogleheads wiki and Vanguard research
---

Investors can hold bonds directly or through funds — each with trade-offs.

**Individual bonds** pay a known coupon and return face value at maturity (absent default). This predictability appeals to investors funding a specific future expense — a technique called a **bond ladder**: buying bonds maturing in successive years (2027, 2028, 2029…) so principal becomes available on schedule, with each maturing rung reinvested at current rates.

Drawbacks: meaningful diversification requires dozens of bonds and substantial capital; corporate and muni bonds trade with wide dealer markups for small investors; and managing maturities/reinvestment takes ongoing effort.

**Bond funds and ETFs** hold hundreds or thousands of bonds, offering instant diversification, professional execution, tiny minimums, and automatic reinvestment. The trade-off: a fund never "matures" — its value fluctuates continuously with interest rates, so there's no date when you're guaranteed your principal back.

**Practical guidance:**

- For most investors, a low-cost **total bond market index fund** is the simplest, most effective choice.
- Treasuries are an exception where individual bonds are easy: no credit risk, no markup via TreasuryDirect or auctions.
- A useful rule: hold a bond fund whose **duration is shorter than your time horizon**; then rising rates help you (via reinvestment at higher yields) more than they hurt.
