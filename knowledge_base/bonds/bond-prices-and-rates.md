---
title: Why Bond Prices Fall When Interest Rates Rise
category: bonds
source: Adapted from FINRA and Investopedia fixed-income guides
---

The most important — and least intuitive — rule in bond investing: **bond prices move opposite to interest rates.**

**The logic:** Suppose you own a bond paying a 3% coupon. If new bonds now pay 5%, nobody will buy yours at full price — why accept 3% when 5% is available? Your bond's market price must drop until its effective yield matches the new 5% environment. Conversely, when rates fall, existing higher-coupon bonds become more valuable.

**Duration measures the sensitivity.** A bond (or bond fund) with a duration of 6 years will lose roughly 6% of its value for every 1 percentage point rise in rates, and gain ~6% for every 1 point fall. Long-term bonds have high durations (15–20), short-term bonds low ones (1–3).

**2022 made this real:** As the Federal Reserve raised rates rapidly, the broad U.S. bond market fell about 13% — its worst year in modern history — and long-term Treasuries fell roughly 30%. "Safe" is relative to maturity.

**What this means in practice:**

- Match bond duration to your time horizon; short-term money belongs in short-duration instruments.
- If you hold an individual bond to maturity, interim price swings don't matter — you still receive face value (absent default).
- Rising rates hurt bond funds at first but eventually help: the fund reinvests at the new, higher yields.
