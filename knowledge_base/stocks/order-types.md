---
title: "Stock Order Types: Market, Limit, and Stop Orders"
category: stocks
source: Adapted from FINRA and SEC investor.gov guidance
---

When you buy or sell a stock, the order type controls how and at what price the trade executes.

**Market order:** Execute immediately at the best available price. Fast and virtually guaranteed to fill, but the price isn't locked — for large, liquid stocks the difference is pennies; for thin stocks it can be substantial. Suitable default for long-term investors trading major stocks and ETFs during market hours.

**Limit order:** Execute only at your specified price or better. A buy limit at $50 will never pay more than $50. You control price but risk no execution if the market never reaches your limit. Recommended for less liquid securities, volatile conditions, or pre/after-hours trading.

**Stop order (stop-loss):** Becomes a market order once the price hits your stop level. A stop at $45 on a $50 stock aims to cap losses near 10%. Caution: in a sharp gap down (e.g., overnight bad news), the fill can be well below the stop price. Stops can also trigger on brief dips, selling you out of a position that recovers an hour later.

**Stop-limit order:** Triggers like a stop but becomes a limit order, protecting fill price at the risk of not executing at all during a crash.

**For long-term investors:** order sophistication matters far less than time in market. Most index-fund investors only ever need market or limit orders, and "set a stop-loss on a 30-year holding" is generally counterproductive.
