---
title: Growth vs. Value Investing
category: stocks
source: Adapted from Investopedia and academic factor research (Fama-French)
---

Growth and value are the two classic styles of stock investing.

**Growth investing** targets companies expanding revenue and earnings faster than average — think technology firms. Investors willingly pay high valuation multiples (P/E of 30, 50, or more) betting that rapid growth will justify the price. The risk: when growth disappoints, these stocks can fall hard, since the premium price assumed perfection.

**Value investing** targets companies trading cheaply relative to their fundamentals — low P/E, low price-to-book, often with solid dividends. These are frequently unglamorous businesses (banks, energy, industrials) or companies facing temporary trouble. The approach, championed by Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett, seeks a "margin of safety": paying $0.70 for $1.00 of value. The risk: some cheap stocks are cheap for good reason ("value traps").

**What history shows:** Academic research (Fama-French) found that value stocks outperformed growth over most of the 20th century. However, growth dominated by a wide margin during the 2010s, driven by large tech. Leadership rotates unpredictably between the styles, sometimes for a decade at a time.

**For beginners:** A total market or S&P 500 index fund holds both styles at market weight, sidestepping the need to pick a side. Style-specific funds (e.g., value ETFs) exist for investors who want a deliberate tilt — but the tilt should be a long-term commitment, not a chase of recent winners.
