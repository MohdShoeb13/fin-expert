title: What Is a Stock?
category: stocks

A stock (also called a share or equity) is a unit of ownership in a corporation. Owning stock makes you a shareholder — a part-owner entitled to a slice of the company's assets and earnings.

**How you make money with stocks:**

1. **Capital appreciation:** The share price rises as the company grows. Buy at $50, sell at $80, and you've earned $30 per share.
2. **Dividends:** Many established companies distribute a portion of profits to shareholders in cash, typically quarterly.

**Why stock prices move:** In the short run, prices reflect supply and demand driven by news, earnings reports, interest rates, and sentiment. In the long run, prices track company earnings — a business that reliably grows its profits will generally see its stock grow too.

**Common vs. preferred stock:** Common stock carries voting rights and unlimited upside but stands last in line if the company is liquidated. Preferred stock pays fixed dividends and has priority over common stock, behaving somewhat like a bond.

**Risks:** Individual stocks can lose most or all of their value — even famous companies (Enron, Lehman Brothers, Kodak) have collapsed. This is why beginners are usually advised to own stocks through diversified funds holding hundreds of companies, rather than betting on a handful of individual names.
