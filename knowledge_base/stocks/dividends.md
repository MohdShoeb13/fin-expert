---
title: Dividends Explained
category: stocks
source: Adapted from Investopedia

A dividend is a cash payment a company makes to its shareholders, usually quarterly, as a way of returning profits to owners.

**Key terms:**

- **Dividend yield:** Annual dividends per share ÷ share price. A $100 stock paying $3/year yields 3%.
- **Ex-dividend date:** You must own the stock before this date to receive the next payment.
- **Payout ratio:** The share of earnings paid as dividends. Very high ratios (90%+) can signal an unsustainable dividend.
- **Dividend aristocrats:** S&P 500 companies that have raised dividends for 25+ consecutive years.

**Why companies pay them:** Mature, profitable businesses (utilities, consumer staples, banks) often generate more cash than they can productively reinvest. Younger growth companies typically pay nothing, preferring to reinvest in expansion.

**Reinvestment is powerful:** Automatically reinvesting dividends (a DRIP — dividend reinvestment plan) buys more shares that themselves generate dividends. Historically, reinvested dividends account for a substantial portion of the stock market's total long-term return.

**Cautions:** A very high yield is often a warning sign — it may reflect a collapsed share price and a dividend about to be cut. Dividends are also not guaranteed; companies reduced or suspended them widely in 2008 and 2020. And in taxable accounts, dividends are taxed in the year received even if reinvested.
