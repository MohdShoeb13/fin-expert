title: How Stock Markets Work

A stock market is an organized marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares. The major U.S. venues are the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the Nasdaq.

**Price discovery:** A stock's price is simply the last price at which a buyer and seller agreed to trade. Continuous auctions match the highest price buyers will pay (the **bid**) with the lowest price sellers will accept (the **ask**). The gap between them is the **spread** — tiny for large companies, wider for thinly traded ones.

**Market indexes** summarize the market's overall movement:

- **S&P 500:** 500 of the largest U.S. companies — the standard benchmark for "the market."
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average:** 30 large companies; older and price-weighted, so less representative.
- **Nasdaq Composite:** All Nasdaq-listed stocks, tilted toward technology.

**Market hours:** Regular U.S. trading runs 9:30 AM–4:00 PM Eastern, Monday–Friday. Pre-market and after-hours sessions exist but have thinner volume and wider spreads.

**Bull and bear markets:** A bull market is a sustained rise; a bear market is a decline of 20% or more from a peak. Since 1950, bear markets have occurred roughly every 5–7 years and lasted about a year on average, while bull markets have lasted far longer — which is why long-term investors are rewarded for patience rather than prediction.
