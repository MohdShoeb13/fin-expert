title: "Rebalancing: Maintaining Your Risk Level"
category: risk_diversification

Rebalancing is periodically restoring your portfolio to its target allocation after market moves push it off course. It is how an allocation remains a decision rather than an accident.

**The drift problem:** Start at 60% stocks / 40% bonds. After a strong bull market, you may sit at 75/25 — silently carrying far more risk than you chose, right when valuations are highest. After a crash you might be at 50/50 — under-risked exactly when expected returns are best.

**How to rebalance:** Sell the overweight asset, buy the underweight one — mechanically buying low and selling high, with zero forecasting required.

**Common approaches:**

- **Calendar:** Once or twice a year (anniversary, birthday). Simple and sufficient.
- **Threshold (bands):** Act only when an asset drifts ±5 percentage points from target.
- **Cash-flow rebalancing (gentlest):** Direct new contributions — and dividends — toward whatever is underweight. Avoids selling and the taxes that come with it; often enough by itself during accumulation years.

**Tax-smart execution:** Rebalance freely inside 401(k)s/IRAs (no tax consequences). In taxable accounts, prefer cash-flow rebalancing, or pair sales with tax-loss harvesting.

**What the research says:** Rebalancing is primarily *risk control*, not return enhancement — Vanguard finds frequency barely affects long-run returns, but unrebalanced portfolios steadily morph into riskier ones. Once or twice a year is plenty; daily fiddling is noise.

The quiet superpower: rebalancing forces you to buy assets everyone hates and trim ones everyone loves — institutionalized contrarianism on autopilot.
