Asset allocation is your portfolio's division among stocks, bonds, and cash. Landmark research (Brinson, Hood & Beebower) found allocation explains the overwhelming majority of a portfolio's return *variability* — making it far more consequential than which particular funds you pick or when you buy.

**Why it's the master decision:** Stocks drive growth; bonds dampen swings. The blend sets both your expected return and your worst-case drawdown:

| Allocation (stocks/bonds) | Historical worst year (approx.) | Character |
|---|---|---|
| 100/0 | −43% | Maximum growth, brutal crashes |
| 80/20 | −34% | Growth-oriented |
| 60/40 | −27% | The classic "balanced" portfolio |
| 40/60 | −18% | Conservative |
| 20/80 | −10% | Capital preservation |

**Choosing yours — three inputs:**

1. **Time horizon:** Decades away → stock-heavy; money needed soon → bond/cash-heavy.
2. **Risk tolerance:** The honest question is "what loss would make me sell everything?" Your allocation must keep your worst case inside that line — an aggressive portfolio you abandon in a crash performs worse than a moderate one you keep.
3. **Need to take risk:** Already wealthy relative to goals? You can afford to dial down.

**Rules of thumb (starting points, not prescriptions):** "age in bonds" (a 30-year-old holds 30% bonds) is traditional; "110 or 120 minus age" in stocks reflects longer lifespans. Target-date funds implement the glide automatically.

Pick an allocation you can hold through a 2008. Then the rest of investing is mostly maintenance.
