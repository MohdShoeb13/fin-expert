title: "Diversification: The Only Free Lunch"

Diversification — spreading money across many investments — is famously called "the only free lunch in investing" (attributed to Harry Markowitz) because it reduces risk *without* proportionally reducing expected return.

**Why it works:** Different assets don't fail together. When one stock collapses, 499 others in an index fund carry on. When U.S. stocks stagnate (as in 2000–2009, the "lost decade"), international stocks or bonds may perform. The math: combining assets whose returns are imperfectly **correlated** produces a smoother ride than any single asset.

**The layers of diversification:**

1. **Across companies:** 500+ stocks instead of 5. Eliminates single-company catastrophe.
2. **Across sectors:** Tech, healthcare, energy, finance — so one industry's winter doesn't freeze the portfolio.
3. **Across geographies:** U.S. + international developed + emerging markets. Leadership rotates by decade.
4. **Across asset classes:** Stocks + bonds (+ cash, real estate) — the most powerful layer, since high-quality bonds often *rise* during stock crashes.
5. **Across time:** Regular contributions diversify your entry prices.

**What diversification cannot do:** save you from broad market declines (in 2008 nearly all stocks fell together — only bonds cushioned), or guarantee gains. And it has a humble corollary — you will *always* own something that's underperforming. That's the proof it's working.

**The classic warning case:** employees holding most of their wealth in employer stock (Enron, 2001) — losing job and savings to the same event. Concentration builds fortunes rarely; it destroys them regularly.
