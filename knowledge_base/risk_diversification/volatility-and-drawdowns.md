title: "Volatility, Drawdowns, and Staying the Course"

---

Volatility — the day-to-day and year-to-year swing in prices — is the toll the market charges for its long-term returns. Knowing what "normal" looks like inoculates against panic.

**What normal actually looks like (U.S. stock market history):**

- An **intra-year drop of ~14% on average** happens in *most* years — even years that finish positive.
- **Corrections** (−10%) arrive roughly every 1–2 years.
- **Bear markets** (−20% or worse) arrive roughly every 5–7 years and last about a year on average.
- **Crashes of −30% to −50%** happen a few times per investing lifetime (2000–02: −49%; 2008–09: −57%; 2020: −34% in five weeks).

Every one of these declines, so far, was eventually followed by new all-time highs — but recoveries took anywhere from months (2020) to over a decade (1929, 2000 for the Nasdaq).

**Drawdown math worth internalizing:** losses require outsized gains to repair — a 50% loss needs a +100% gain to break even. This asymmetry is why allocation (holding bonds) matters and why retirees can't simply "wait it out" like 30-year-olds.

**The cost of flinching:** missing just the 10 best market days over 20 years roughly halves total returns — and the best days cluster *inside* bear markets, unreachable to anyone hiding in cash.

**Practical conclusions:** expect declines as scheduled weather, not emergencies; size your stock allocation so the historical worst case is survivable; automate contributions so volatility becomes your discount program rather than your fear trigger.
