---
title: Traditional IRA Explained
category: retirement
source: Adapted from IRS Publication 590-A/B
---

A Traditional IRA (Individual Retirement Arrangement) is a personal retirement account you open yourself at any broker — no employer needed — offering tax-deferred growth.

**The tax deal:** Contributions may be tax-deductible now (reducing this year's taxable income), investments grow untaxed for decades, and withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income. In essence: pay taxes later, ideally at a lower retirement tax rate.

**Contribution limits (2025):** $7,000 per year ($8,000 if 50+), shared across all your IRAs (Traditional + Roth combined). You need earned income at least equal to the contribution.

**The deductibility catch:** If you (or your spouse) are covered by a workplace plan like a 401(k), the deduction phases out above certain income levels. High earners covered by a 401(k) may get no deduction — in which case a Roth IRA or the "backdoor Roth" strategy usually makes more sense.

**Withdrawal rules:**

- Before 59½: taxes plus a 10% penalty, with exceptions (first home up to $10,000, qualified education, certain hardships).
- **RMDs (Required Minimum Distributions):** Starting at age 73, the IRS forces annual withdrawals based on life expectancy — you cannot defer taxes forever.

**Traditional vs. Roth in one line:** Deduct now and pay taxes in retirement (Traditional) versus pay taxes now and withdraw tax-free later (Roth). Lower expected retirement tax bracket favors Traditional; higher favors Roth.
