---
title: "HSAs: The Triple Tax Advantage"
category: retirement
source: Adapted from IRS Publication 969
---

A Health Savings Account (HSA) is nominally for medical expenses, but it's also the only account in the U.S. tax code with a **triple tax advantage** — making it a stealth retirement account for those who can use one.

**The triple advantage:**

1. Contributions are tax-deductible (and avoid payroll tax if made via payroll).
2. Growth is tax-free.
3. Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free — at any age.

No other account (401(k), IRA, Roth) offers all three.

**Eligibility:** You must be enrolled in a qualified High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). 2025 contribution limits: $4,300 individual / $8,550 family, plus $1,000 catch-up at 55+.

**The power move — invest, don't spend:** Most HSA providers allow investing the balance in index funds. The optimized strategy: pay current medical bills out of pocket, let the HSA compound for decades, and keep receipts — qualified expenses can be reimbursed *years later* with no time limit, effectively making the HSA withdrawable tax-free on demand once you've accumulated receipts.

**Retirement behavior:** After 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed like a Traditional IRA (no penalty), and medical costs in retirement are substantial anyway — Fidelity estimates a retired couple needs roughly $300,000+ for healthcare.

**Caution:** HSA funds spent on non-qualified expenses before 65 incur income tax plus a 20% penalty. Also unlike an FSA, HSA money never expires — it's yours for life.
