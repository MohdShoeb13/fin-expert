---
title: Retirement Accounts When Changing Jobs
category: retirement
source: Adapted from IRS rollover guidance and FINRA investor alerts
---

When you leave a job, your 401(k) balance has four possible destinations — and one of them is a costly mistake.

**Option 1 — Roll over to an IRA (most common recommendation):** A direct rollover to a Traditional IRA preserves all tax benefits, is not a taxable event, and typically upgrades you from a limited 401(k) menu to the full universe of low-cost funds.

**Option 2 — Roll into the new employer's 401(k):** Keeps everything consolidated in one workplace plan. Worthwhile if the new plan has good low-cost funds. Also preserves the IRA "backdoor Roth" strategy for high earners (large pre-tax IRA balances complicate it).

**Option 3 — Leave it in the old plan:** Allowed if the balance exceeds $7,000. Fine if the plan is excellent (some mega-employer plans have institutional pricing), but easy to lose track of — billions sit in forgotten accounts.

**Option 4 — Cash out (the mistake):** Triggers income tax on the full balance plus a 10% penalty if under 59½, and permanently destroys decades of compounding. A $30,000 cash-out at 30 could have grown to over $300,000 by retirement.

**Critical execution detail:** Always request a **direct (trustee-to-trustee) rollover** where the check goes straight between institutions. If the check is made out to you personally, 20% is withheld and you have only 60 days to redeposit the *full* amount or it counts as a taxable distribution.
