How much do you need to retire? The most-cited answer comes from the **4% rule**, based on William Bengen's research and the Trinity Study.

**The rule:** In your first retirement year, withdraw 4% of your portfolio; each following year, adjust that dollar amount for inflation. Historically, a 50–75% stock portfolio survived every 30-year U.S. retirement period since the 1920s under this rule — including retirements starting right before the Great Depression.

**The flip side — the 25x rule:** If you can live on 4% of your portfolio, you need **25 times your annual expenses** to retire. Spending $60,000/year implies a $1.5 million target. Note it's driven by your *expenses*, not your income — a frugal lifestyle both saves faster and needs less.

**Savings benchmarks along the way** (commonly cited, e.g., by Fidelity): 1× salary saved by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60, 10× by 67.

**Honest caveats:**

- 4% is a planning guideline derived from U.S. historical data, not a guarantee; some researchers suggest 3.3–3.8% for longer retirements or expensive markets.
- Flexibility beats rigidity: trimming withdrawals in bad market years dramatically improves survival odds.
- Social Security, pensions, and part-time income all reduce the portfolio you actually need.

The deeper lesson: retirement readiness is a ratio of assets to spending — and your savings *rate* is the lever you control most directly.
