---
title: "Roth IRA: Tax-Free Growth"
category: retirement
source: Adapted from IRS Publication 590-A/B
---

A Roth IRA flips the traditional tax deal: you contribute money that's already been taxed, and in exchange, **all growth and qualified withdrawals are completely tax-free**.

**Why it's often called the best account in personal finance:**

- Decades of compounding, never taxed again. $7,000/year invested from age 25 could exceed $1.5 million by 65 — all withdrawable tax-free.
- **Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime, tax- and penalty-free**, giving rare flexibility.
- **No RMDs:** Unlike Traditional IRAs and 401(k)s, the IRS never forces withdrawals; the account can keep growing for life and passes income-tax-free to heirs.
- Tax-free withdrawals don't raise your retirement income for purposes of Social Security taxation or Medicare premiums.

**Limits and eligibility (2025):** $7,000/year ($8,000 if 50+), shared with Traditional IRA contributions. Direct contributions phase out at higher incomes (starting around $150,000 single / $236,000 married filing jointly). Above the limits, the **backdoor Roth** (contribute to a non-deductible Traditional IRA, then convert) is a widely used, legal workaround.

**Qualified withdrawal of earnings** requires age 59½ *and* the account being open 5+ years.

**Who benefits most:** Young investors and anyone expecting an equal-or-higher tax bracket in retirement — paying today's tax rate on the seed beats paying tomorrow's rate on the full-grown tree.
