---
title: "The Employer Match: Free Money First"
category: retirement
source: Adapted from DOL guidance and standard financial planning practice
---

An employer match is money your company adds to your 401(k) based on what you contribute — the closest thing to free money in personal finance.

**Common formulas:**

- "100% match on the first 4% of salary": contribute 4%, employer adds another 4%.
- "50% match on the first 6%": contribute 6%, employer adds 3%.

On a $60,000 salary with a 100%-up-to-4% match, contributing $2,400 gets you another $2,400 — an instant, guaranteed 100% return before any market growth. Failing to capture the match is leaving part of your compensation unclaimed; surveys repeatedly find about a quarter of employees do exactly that.

**Vesting:** Employer contributions may vest over time (e.g., 25% per year over 4 years). Leave before fully vested and you forfeit the unvested portion — your own contributions are always 100% yours. Check the vesting schedule before job changes.

**Priority order used by most planners:**

1. Contribute enough to capture the **full match** — before anything else.
2. Pay off high-interest debt (credit cards).
3. Build the emergency fund.
4. Max a Roth or Traditional IRA (often better fund choices and lower fees than 401(k) menus).
5. Return to the 401(k) and contribute toward its annual maximum.

The match also compounds: that $2,400 of employer money, invested for 30 years at 7%, becomes roughly $18,000 — per year of matching.
