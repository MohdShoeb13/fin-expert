title: 401(k) Basics

A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement account that lets you invest part of each paycheck with major tax advantages. It is the backbone of retirement saving for most American workers.

**How it works:** You choose a contribution percentage; the money is deducted from payroll automatically and invested in funds you select from the plan's menu (typically index funds and target-date funds).

**Traditional vs. Roth 401(k):**

- **Traditional:** Contributions are pre-tax (reducing this year's taxable income); withdrawals in retirement are taxed as income.
- **Roth:** Contributions are after-tax; qualified withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free, including all growth.

**Contribution limits (2025):** $23,500 of your own money per year, plus a $7,500 catch-up if you're 50+. Employer contributions don't count against your limit.

**The employer match is the headline benefit:** A typical match is 50–100% of your contributions up to 3–6% of salary. A 100% match is an instant, guaranteed 100% return — no other investment comes close. At minimum, always contribute enough to capture the full match.

**Rules to know:** Withdrawals before age 59½ generally incur a 10% penalty plus taxes (with limited exceptions). Money grows tax-deferred while invested. When changing jobs, you can roll your balance into the new employer's plan or an IRA without taxes — don't cash out, which triggers taxes, penalties, and lost compounding.
