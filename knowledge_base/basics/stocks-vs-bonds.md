Stocks and bonds are the two fundamental asset classes in most portfolios, and they play very different roles.

**Stocks (equities)** represent ownership in a company. When you buy a share of Apple, you own a tiny fraction of the business and share in its profits through price appreciation and dividends. Stocks offer higher expected returns (historically ~7–10% annually for U.S. markets) but with high volatility — drops of 30–50% happen in severe bear markets.

**Bonds (fixed income)** are loans you make to a government or corporation. In exchange, the borrower pays you regular interest (the "coupon") and returns your principal at maturity. Bonds offer lower expected returns (historically ~3–5%) but much steadier value, making them a portfolio's shock absorber.

**Key differences:**

| Feature | Stocks | Bonds |
|---|---|---|
| What you own | A piece of a company | A loan/IOU |
| Primary return | Price growth + dividends | Interest payments |
| Risk | High volatility | Lower (but not zero) |
| Role in portfolio | Growth engine | Stability and income |

A classic starting point is a mix like 60% stocks / 40% bonds, adjusted for age and risk tolerance: more stocks when young (decades to recover from crashes), more bonds approaching retirement (less time to recover, more need for stability).
