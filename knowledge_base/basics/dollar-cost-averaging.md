---
title: Dollar-Cost Averaging
category: basics
source: Adapted from Investopedia
---

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals — say $500 on the first of every month — regardless of what the market is doing.

**How it helps:** Because the amount is fixed, you automatically buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. If a fund costs $100 in January you buy 5 shares with $500; if it drops to $50 in February you buy 10. Your average cost per share ends up below the average price you observed.

**The real benefits are behavioral:**

- It removes the impossible decision of "when is the right time to buy?"
- It prevents panic and paralysis during downturns — your plan keeps executing.
- It pairs naturally with paychecks: most 401(k) contributions are DCA by default.
- It builds the habit of consistent investing, which matters more than timing.

**DCA vs. lump sum:** If you receive a windfall, studies (notably from Vanguard) show investing it immediately beats DCA about two-thirds of the time, because markets rise more often than they fall. But DCA-ing a windfall over 6–12 months is a reasonable choice if a sudden 20% drop right after investing would cause you to panic-sell. The worst option is waiting in cash for a "better time" that never feels right.
