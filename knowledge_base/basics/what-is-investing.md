title: What Is Investing?
category: basics
source: Adapted from SEC investor.gov educational materials
---

Investing means putting money to work in assets — such as stocks, bonds, or funds — with the expectation that it will grow over time. Unlike saving, where money sits safely in a bank account earning modest interest, investing accepts some risk of loss in exchange for the potential of higher long-term returns.

The core trade-off in investing is **risk versus return**. Historically, the U.S. stock market has returned about 7–10% per year on average over long periods, but with significant year-to-year swings, including occasional drops of 20% or more. Bank savings accounts, by contrast, rarely outpace inflation.

Key principles for beginners:

- **Start early.** Time is the most powerful force in investing because returns compound.
- **Invest regularly.** Contributing a fixed amount on a schedule smooths out market ups and downs.
- **Diversify.** Spreading money across many investments reduces the impact of any single one failing.
- **Keep costs low.** Fees compound against you just as returns compound for you.
- **Stay invested.** Trying to time the market usually hurts returns; missing just a handful of the best days can dramatically reduce long-term gains.

Investing is not gambling when done with diversified holdings and a long time horizon — it is participating in the long-term growth of the economy.
