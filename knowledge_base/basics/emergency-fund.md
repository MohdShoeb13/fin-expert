---
title: Build an Emergency Fund Before Investing
category: basics
source: Adapted from Bogleheads wiki and CFPB guidance
---

Before investing a single dollar in the stock market, financial planners almost universally recommend building an **emergency fund**: cash set aside for unexpected expenses like job loss, medical bills, or car repairs.

**How much?** The standard guideline is 3–6 months of essential living expenses. Lean toward 6+ months if your income is variable (freelancing, commission-based) or you're the sole earner in a household; 3 months may suffice for dual-income households with stable jobs.

**Where to keep it:** The fund must be safe and liquid — a high-yield savings account or money market fund. It should NOT be invested in stocks, because emergencies don't wait for market recoveries. Imagine needing the money in March 2020 when markets had just dropped 34%.

**Why it matters for investors:** An emergency fund is what lets you leave investments untouched during a crisis. Without one, a job loss during a bear market forces you to sell stocks at the worst possible time, converting temporary paper losses into permanent ones. It also protects you from high-interest credit card debt, which at 20%+ APR outpaces any realistic investment return.

**Order of operations** commonly recommended: (1) small starter emergency fund, (2) employer 401(k) match — it's free money, (3) pay off high-interest debt, (4) full emergency fund, (5) then invest for long-term goals.
