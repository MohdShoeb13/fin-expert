---
title: Compound Interest and Compound Growth
category: basics
source: Adapted from SEC investor.gov and standard finance texts
---

Compound interest is earning returns on your returns. When your investment gains are reinvested, they generate their own gains, creating exponential growth over time. Albert Einstein allegedly called it "the eighth wonder of the world."

**How it works:** If you invest $1,000 at 8% annual return, you earn $80 in year one. In year two, you earn 8% on $1,080 — that's $86.40. The gap widens every year. After 30 years, that single $1,000 grows to about $10,063 without you adding a cent.

**The formula:** Future Value = P × (1 + r)^n, where P is the principal, r is the annual rate of return, and n is the number of years.

**The Rule of 72:** Divide 72 by your annual return rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double. At 8%, money doubles roughly every 9 years.

**Why starting early matters:** An investor who contributes $200/month from age 25 to 35 and then stops (total: $24,000) can end up with more at retirement than one who contributes $200/month from age 35 to 65 (total: $72,000), assuming equal returns. The extra decade of compounding outweighs the extra contributions.

Compounding also works against you with debt and fees: a 1% higher annual fee can consume roughly a quarter of a portfolio's value over 40 years.
