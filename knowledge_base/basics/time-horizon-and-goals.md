---
title: Time Horizon and Goal-Based Investing
category: basics
source: Adapted from CFP Board and Bogleheads wiki principles
---

Your **time horizon** — when you'll need the money — is the single most important factor in deciding how to invest it.

**Short-term goals (0–3 years):** House down payment next year, upcoming tuition, emergency fund. This money belongs in cash equivalents: high-yield savings, money market funds, CDs, or short-term Treasuries. Stocks are inappropriate because a market drop could arrive exactly when you need the money, and 1–3 years is often not enough time to recover.

**Medium-term goals (3–10 years):** A house in five years, a sabbatical. A balanced mix — for example 40–60% stocks with the rest in bonds — accepts moderate risk for moderate growth.

**Long-term goals (10+ years):** Retirement decades away, a newborn's college fund. Long horizons can ride out multiple market cycles, so stock-heavy allocations (80–100% equities) are common and historically rewarded. Every 15-year period in modern U.S. market history has produced positive stock returns.

**Matching is the discipline:** the most common beginner errors are investing short-term money aggressively (forced selling in a crash) and investing long-term money too conservatively (inflation quietly erodes it).

As a goal approaches, glide the allocation toward safety — this is exactly what target-date funds automate for retirement.
