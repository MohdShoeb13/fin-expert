title: Opening Your First Brokerage Account
category: basics
source: Adapted from SEC investor.gov and FINRA guidance
---

A brokerage account is the gateway to investing — an account at a licensed firm that lets you buy and sell stocks, bonds, ETFs, and mutual funds.

**Types of accounts:**

- **Taxable brokerage account:** No contribution limits or withdrawal restrictions, but you owe taxes on dividends and realized gains each year.
- **Retirement accounts (IRA, Roth IRA):** Tax advantages in exchange for leaving money until retirement age (generally 59½).

**What to look for in a broker:**

- **Zero commissions** on stock/ETF trades (now standard at major U.S. brokers).
- **No account minimums or maintenance fees.**
- **Fractional shares**, which let you invest $50 in a stock trading at $500.
- **SIPC insurance**, protecting up to $500,000 in securities if the broker fails (this does not protect against market losses).
- **Good index fund selection** with low expense ratios.

**Steps to start:** (1) choose a reputable broker, (2) complete the online application (identity and tax info, ~15 minutes), (3) link your bank account and transfer funds, (4) place your first order — many beginners start with a broad, diversified index fund or target-date fund rather than individual stocks.

Avoid brokers or platforms promising guaranteed returns, and be wary of pressure to trade frequently — activity generates costs, not returns.
