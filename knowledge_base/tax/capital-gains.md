---
title: Capital Gains Tax Basics
category: tax
source: Adapted from IRS Topic 409 and Investopedia
---

A capital gain is the profit from selling an investment for more than you paid. Crucially, gains are only taxed when **realized** — when you actually sell. An investment that grows for 30 years untouched generates no tax bill along the way (dividends aside).

**The holding-period distinction is the big one:**

- **Short-term gains** (held one year or less): taxed at ordinary income rates — 10% to 37%.
- **Long-term gains** (held more than one year): taxed at preferential rates — **0%, 15%, or 20%** depending on income. For 2025, the 0% bracket covers taxable income up to roughly $48,000 (single) / $97,000 (married filing jointly).

Holding just one day past the one-year mark can cut the tax on a gain nearly in half for a middle-bracket investor.

**Other key mechanics:**

- **Cost basis:** What you paid, including reinvested dividends. Track it; it determines your gain.
- **Capital losses offset gains**, and up to $3,000 of excess loss offsets ordinary income per year, with the remainder carried forward indefinitely.
- **High earners** pay an extra 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax above ~$200,000 (single) / $250,000 (married).
- **Stepped-up basis:** Inherited assets reset their basis to value at death — embedded gains are forgiven.

**Behavioral takeaway:** Frequent trading converts would-be long-term gains into higher-taxed short-term ones — one more way buy-and-hold quietly outperforms.
