---
title: Tax-Loss Harvesting
category: tax
source: Adapted from IRS rules and Bogleheads wiki
---

Tax-loss harvesting means deliberately selling an investment at a loss to capture a tax benefit, while keeping your money invested in something similar.

**How it works:** Suppose you bought an international index fund for $20,000 and it's now worth $16,000. Sell it, **realize the $4,000 loss**, and immediately buy a *different but similar* fund (say, another broad international index tracking a different benchmark). Your portfolio exposure barely changes, but you now have a $4,000 loss for tax purposes.

**What losses are worth:**

- They offset realized capital gains dollar-for-dollar (short-term losses first against short-term gains — the highest-taxed kind).
- Up to **$3,000 per year** of excess loss deducts against ordinary income.
- Unused losses **carry forward indefinitely**.

**The wash-sale rule is the trap:** If you buy the same or a "substantially identical" security within **30 days before or after** the sale (in *any* of your accounts, including IRAs and a spouse's accounts), the loss is disallowed. This is why harvesters swap into a *similar-but-not-identical* fund rather than repurchasing the same one.

**Caveats:** Harvesting lowers your cost basis, so it partly *defers* tax rather than eliminating it (still valuable — deferral plus the rate gap between income and long-term gains rates). It only applies in taxable accounts — there are no losses to harvest inside IRAs or 401(k)s. Don't let the tax tail wag the investment dog: never sell into a meaningfully different exposure just for a deduction.
