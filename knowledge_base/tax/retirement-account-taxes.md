---
title: "Taxes on Retirement Accounts: Contributions, Withdrawals, RMDs"
category: tax
source: Adapted from IRS Publications 590-B and 575
---

Retirement accounts defer or eliminate taxes — but each type settles with the IRS differently.

**Traditional 401(k)/IRA — taxed on the way out:**

- Withdrawals are ordinary income at your then-current marginal rate.
- Before 59½: an additional 10% early-withdrawal penalty, with exceptions (disability, certain medical costs, first home for IRAs up to $10,000, substantially equal periodic payments, the "rule of 55" for 401(k)s after leaving a job at 55+).
- **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** begin at age 73: the IRS mandates annual withdrawals based on account balance and life expectancy. Missing an RMD incurs a 25% excise tax on the shortfall.

**Roth IRA — taxed on the way in:**

- Direct contributions come out anytime, tax- and penalty-free.
- Earnings come out tax-free once you're 59½ *and* past the 5-year rule.
- No RMDs during the owner's lifetime.

**Strategies that arise from these rules:**

- **Roth conversions:** In low-income years (early retirement, sabbaticals), convert Traditional dollars to Roth, paying tax at temporarily low rates and shrinking future RMDs.
- **Withdrawal sequencing:** Conventional order is taxable → Traditional → Roth, letting tax-free money compound longest (individual situations vary).
- **Inherited accounts:** Most non-spouse heirs must empty inherited IRAs within 10 years (SECURE Act), making Roth assets especially valuable to leave behind.

The unifying principle: you control *when* retirement money is taxed — and timing withdrawals into low-rate years is one of the largest tax levers most households ever get.
