title: Taxable vs. Tax-Advantaged Accounts (and Asset Location)

*Where* you hold investments can matter nearly as much as *what* you hold.

**The three tax environments:**

1. **Tax-deferred (Traditional 401(k)/IRA):** Deduct now, grow untaxed, pay income tax on withdrawal.
2. **Tax-free (Roth 401(k)/IRA, HSA):** Pay tax now (or never, for HSA medical), grow and withdraw tax-free.
3. **Taxable (brokerage):** No special treatment — dividends and realized gains are taxed yearly — but no contribution limits or withdrawal restrictions either.

**General funding order:** 401(k) to the match → IRA/Roth IRA → max 401(k) → HSA if eligible → taxable brokerage for everything beyond.

**Asset location — placing assets in their best-suited account:**

- **Bonds and REITs** generate interest/dividends taxed as ordinary income → best in **tax-deferred** accounts.
- **Total-market stock index funds** are naturally tax-efficient (low turnover, qualified dividends) → fine in **taxable** accounts.
- **Highest-growth assets** benefit most from the Roth's tax-free compounding.

**Taxable-account perks worth knowing:** long-term gains rates can be as low as 0%, losses can be harvested, money is accessible at any age (important for early retirement), and heirs receive stepped-up basis.

A reasonable simplification for beginners: max the advantaged accounts first, keep tax-inefficient assets out of taxable accounts, and don't let tax optimization delay actually investing.
